How it works

Nobody has to look at a screen.

Put the phone where it can hear everyone, tap Start, and play. Up to four players take turns: kids, parents, grandparents.

1

It asks out loud

"Dad, what's 9 times 6?" A natural voice built right into the app reads every question, no setup needed.

2

You say the answer

Just say "fifty-four." The app listens, scores it, and moves on. Say "repeat", "skip" or "pause" any time.

3

It teaches as you go

Miss one and you'll hear the answer plus a trick to remember it. That fact comes back a few turns later.

A coach for every player

Hard enough to learn.
Easy enough to keep winning.

The app learns what each player knows and picks questions they'll get right about 85% of the time, the sweet spot for learning.

  • Beginners start with the easy facts: 2s, 5s and 10s.
  • Experts get the tough ones, like 7 × 8 and 12 × 12, and a faster answer clock.
  • Variety built in. No number keeps coming back, and no two players get the same question in a game.
⚖️

Fair for kids and grown-ups

Everyone plays at their own level, on their own clock. Speed points count down while you think, so a quick answer earns up to 15 points. Facts that are hard for you earn a bonus. A seven-year-old can beat a parent.

🏆

A family leaderboard

See who's on top this week and all time, how many facts each person has mastered, and celebrate milestones like "Maya has mastered the 5s!"

Tips that make it click

Every miss comes with a trick.

Hearing the answer helps. Hearing why helps it stick. There's a trick for every table.

💡 Nines trick: take one away from 7 to get 6. Then 6 plus what makes 9? 3. So it's 63.
💡 Elevens trick: 11 times 4 is just two 4s side by side, 44.
💡 Remember five, six, seven, eight: 56 is 7 times 8!

Why I built this

I never learned my times tables. It has bugged me my whole life. I can do harder stuff, but I still stall on 7×8, and I did not want my son hitting the same wall I did.

The thing that finally made it click for me was simple. You cannot do the harder math if the easy facts are not already in your head. Your brain can only juggle a few things at once. If 7×8 is not already automatic, it has to stop and work out the fact in the middle of the bigger problem, and that is usually where it falls apart. Schools got talked out of memorization for a while. A lot of that got walked back. I wish someone had just had me memorize them.

So I built this so my son and I can play while we are driving, just by talking to it. We compete. A car ride turns into a game instead of a screen. I want him to have an easier time in math than I did, and I want us doing it together.

It was designed to be played by a parent and their child. But the whole family can play together, or you can even practice alone. And if you need to keep it quiet, there is a tap mode so you can answer without talking.

It also follows one of the rules of good teaching: the software adapts to the learner, keeping them engaged but still challenged. It adjusts so each player gets about 85% of their answers right. As you learn more, you get the problems that are harder for you, which reinforces your learning while keeping it fun.

And it is a game. The faster you answer, the more points you get. There is a leaderboard so you can see how you are all doing. And there is even a chart where you can see which times tables you have mastered and which ones you need help on.

This will always be free. It runs on free open-source software. I am not trying to make money on it. I built it for my family. If it helps another kid learn math and actually like it, that is the whole point.

— Travis

Questions

Good to know

Is it really free?

Yes. No ads, no in-app purchases, no subscriptions. It was made by a dad who wanted to learn his times tables alongside his son, and wanted other families to have it too.

What ages is it for?

Anyone learning or brushing up on multiplication, roughly ages 6 and up, including grown-ups. The coach adjusts to each player, so a beginner and an expert can play in the same game.

Does it need internet?

No. The voice is built into the app, and on most devices speech recognition runs on the device too.

What devices does it work on?

iPhone and iPad running iOS 17 or later.

Can we play without talking?

Yes. Choose "Tap it" on the home screen to answer with a keypad instead.

Is progress shared between devices?

Not yet. Each iPhone or iPad keeps its own players and progress.

Privacy policy

We collect nothing.

Last updated: October 2026

The Times Table Road Trip app does not collect, store or share any personal information.

No accounts, ads, tracking or analytics. The app has no servers and sends nothing to us. There are no third-party advertising, analytics or tracking tools in it.

Names and progress stay on your device. Player names, scores and learning progress are saved only on your iPhone or iPad. They're included in your own device backups and erased if you delete the app.

Microphone and speech recognition. The app listens only while a question is being answered, so it can turn your spoken answer into a number. On most devices this happens entirely on the device. On some older devices, Apple's speech recognition may process the audio on Apple's servers under Apple's privacy policy. We never receive it.

The game voice is computer-generated on your device by a built-in voice model. Nothing you say or type is sent anywhere to create it.

The website. You can also play in a browser. Without signing in, scores stay in that browser. Signing in with Google is optional: it saves that player's progress to their account, and school accounts show a first name and last initial on that school's leaderboard. Details.

Children. The app is designed for families. It collects no information from anyone, including children.

Changes. If this policy ever changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date.
